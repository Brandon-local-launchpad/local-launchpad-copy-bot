=== NICHE BLOCK: TREE SURGEON ===

TRADE: arboriculture in England: tree pruning, felling and removal, stump work, hedges, surveys and reports, emergency and storm work, and the clearance and woodland work tree surgeons also do.

SERVICES (one row each in Tables A, B and F; use these exact names):
Tree work: Tree pruning and crown reduction | Tree removal and felling | Tree stump removal | Tree cabling and bracing | Tree planting | Tree transplanting | Pollarding and coppicing | Emergency tree work | Ivy removal
Hedges: Hedge trimming | Hedge reduction and removal
Surveys: Tree surveys and reports | Ash dieback management
Clearance: Site and vegetation clearance | Woodland management | Wood chipping | Firewood and logs | Root barriers

RULES TO COVER IN TABLE C (where they apply):
- BS 3998:2010 (tree work recommendations): what it says about crown reduction (percentage and the move to specifying by metres), crown lifting, thinning, pollarding, pruning cuts, wound treatment, and what "topping" is and why it is not recommended.
- Tree preservation orders: Town and Country Planning Act 1990 part VIII, the Town and Country Planning (Tree Preservation) (England) Regulations 2012, applying for consent, the 8 week decision period, exemptions (dead, dangerous, nuisance in law, fruit trees in some cases, the 5 day notice for dangerous trees), penalties, replacement duties.
- Conservation areas: section 211 notices, 6 weeks, trees over 75 mm diameter at 1.5 m, exemptions.
- Felling licences: the Forestry Act 1967 and the 5 cubic metre per calendar quarter threshold, the 2 cubic metre sale limit, exemptions for gardens, the Forestry Commission's role.
- Wildlife: Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 (nesting birds, no legal season but the commonly observed March to August window), the Conservation of Habitats and Species Regulations 2017 for bats (roost potential surveys, licences), the Protection of Badgers Act, hedgerow regulations.
- Hedgerows Regulations 1997 for countryside hedges, the High Hedges part of the Anti-social Behaviour Act 2003 (what it covers: evergreen, over 2 m, two or more trees; what it does not).
- Boundaries and neighbours: common law right to cut overhanging branches to the boundary, returning cuttings, roots, subsidence claims and tree related subsidence on clay soils (the ABI and the Joint Mitigation Protocol), highways and the Highways Act 1980 on overhanging vegetation.
- Health and safety: the Forestry Industry Safety Accord guides (FISA), HSE AFAG guidance, chainsaw competence (NPTC / City & Guilds Land Based units, the LANTRA equivalents), the Work at Height Regulations and climbing, LOLER for climbing and rigging equipment (6 monthly inspections), PUWER, aerial rescue requirements, MEWP use, working near overhead power lines (ENA guidance, the 1 m and 3 m distances), the Electricity at Work Regulations and who may work near lines, first aid, lone working.
- Tree surveys: BS 5837:2012 (trees in relation to design, demolition and construction) and what a BS 5837 survey is, the categories (A to U), root protection areas; mortgage and insurance tree reports; the Quantified Tree Risk Assessment and VALID methods; the National Tree Safety Group's "Common sense risk management of trees"; the duty of care under the Occupiers' Liability Acts.
- Ash dieback: the Forestry Commission and Tree Council guidance, when ash must be felled, the risk from brittle ash and why standard climbing is unsafe, the Chalara position.
- Pests and diseases: the statutory ones (oak processionary moth control zones, Phytophthora, notifiable pests under the Plant Health Regulation), the duty to report.
- Waste and biomass: duty of care for arisings, waste carrier registration, the position on chipping on site and leaving arisings, firewood and the Ready to Burn scheme (the 2021 wet wood rules, moisture content 20%).
- Insurance: public liability levels commonly required (£5m and £10m for local authority work), professional indemnity for reports, the difference, and the ARB Approved Contractor requirements.
- Consumer: Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, Consumer Rights Act 2015, written quotes and TPO responsibility (who applies).

BODIES FOR TABLE D:
Arboricultural Association (ARB Approved Contractor, Registered Consultant, membership grades), ISA (Certified Arborist), Institute of Chartered Foresters, LANTRA, NPTC / City & Guilds Land Based Services, FISA, National Tree Safety Group, Forestry Commission (felling licences), Tree Council, TrustMark, Trading Standards Buy With Confidence, Checkatrade and Which? what crown reduction, lifting, thinning and topping each mean and what BS 3998 says about each; how much of a crown can be removed at once; the best and worst times of year to prune by species group (oak, beech, cherry/prunus, walnut, maple and birch sap bleeders, fruit trees); whether tree work can be done in nesting season; how to check if a tree has a TPO; the 75 mm conservation area rule; felling licence thresholds; whether a neighbour can cut overhanging branches; what to do with the cuttings; whether a tree can be removed because of subsidence fears; stump grinding depth and whether regrowth occurs; eco plug / stump treatment chemicals and who may apply them; whether ivy harms trees; how ash dieback is identified; whether tree surgeons need a licence (no; what they need instead); what a tree survey is for a mortgage; what "lopping and topping" means on an invoice; how long a felled tree's roots take to decay; whether wood chip can be left on site; firewood seasoning time and the Ready to Burn moisture limit; the ARB Approved Contractor assessment; the two insurance types.
