  What changed from v1
  This is the Build Work homepage spec rebuilt on the design brief's section order, with the
  contradictions fixed and the gaps closed so the copy bot and the builder stop guessing.
  The design brief's structure is kept as it stands; where the brief and the v1 spec
  disagreed, the brief wins and this doc says why.

       Section order follows the design brief: Hero, Trusted & Rated By, Content block,
       Services, Why choose us, Reviews, Process, Meet the owner, Service areas, FAQ, Final
       CTA and footer. The v1 CTA band (old section 8) is dropped, because the brief says we
       don't need the green CTA band.
       H1 is the clean keyword only, with no modifier. The modifier moves to the SEO title, and
       the trust line under the H1 says what the visitor gets. This matches Core 30 (H1 =
       primary category + city) and removes the v1 contradiction between the H1 rule and
       section 1.
       The ownership rule now restricts phrasing, not facts. The guarantee and years can be
       stated on inner pages; only the homepage story, owner bio, general process and
       general FAQs are homepage-only.
       FAQ is visible, not an accordion. The brief says accordion; Core 30 says Google reads
       the rendered mobile page and ignores collapsed content. We keep the accordion look
       (bordered rows) but every answer renders open.
       Why choose us is 4 across on desktop, 2x2 on tablet, stacked on mobile. The brief's
       heading says 2x2 and its body says 4 across; this resolves it.
       Reviews cards show name, stars and text, not town. Google reviews have no town field,
       so the brief's town line is dropped.
       Secondary categories get an inline editorial link in body copy (Caleb's homepage rule),
       inside each service card's copy and in content block paragraph 2, not as a separate
       card link.
       Visible name, address and phone are specced in the footer, character for character
       with the GBP, with a hidden-address rule. v1 only had them in schema.
       Every custom value has an empty-value fallback. Rating display, review-count H2 and
       [COUNTY] now have thresholds and truth checks.

  Scope, ownership and site-wide rules
  Every client gets one homepage, and it is the GBP landing page: the URL in the GBP
  website field. It targets the primary GBP category plus the primary city, e.g. Roofer Bristol.
  Google uses this page to check that the website and the GBP are the same business, so
  the 8 consistency signals (below) are non-negotiable.

  Keyword protection

       The Page Map never creates a Location page for the primary city. The homepage is that
       city's page.
       No Category page H1 may equal the homepage H1. If the highest-volume Ahrefs result
       for the homepage matches a category exactly, broaden the homepage to the trade
       umbrella term (Core 30 rule).

  What the homepage owns (copy that no other page repeats)

       The business story (section 3).
       The owner bio (section 8).
       The general process (section 7).
       The general FAQs: cost overview, insurance, guarantee, start time, quotes, areas
       (section 10).

  What the homepage does not own

  Facts are not duplicate content. The guarantee length, insurance figure, years trading and
  jobs completed may be stated on any page where they help a visitor decide. The rule is:
  never copy the homepage sentence, write a new one. Inner pages pull the figure from the
  custom value and phrase it for that page.

       Section 5 is the one place the numbers are explained (years, guarantee, insurance).
       Section 3 may mention the year founded in passing but never the guarantee length or

  Voice

       First person plural ("we") everywhere, except section 8 which is first person singular
       ("I").
       Natural sentences. No fragments ("Landscaper in Southampton, Eastleigh and the
       surrounding areas." is not a sentence).
       Soft cap of 25 words a sentence. Paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences. No em dashes, en
       dashes or hyphenated compounds ("free draining", not "free-draining"), per the
       Calibration Pack rule; the VA checks for reintroduced dashes before publishing.
       Every paragraph carries at least one concrete, checkable detail (a place, a number, a
       named product, a named process). No paragraph of pure reassurance.
       Talks to the visitor's situation, not about the business. Lead with the problem they
       have, then what we do about it.
       Business name appears in body copy at least once in the hero intro and once in section
       3.

  Examples in this doc

  Every example is marked do-not-reuse. The copy bot writes fresh phrasing from the
  client's confirmed data. If a sentence in a draft matches an example here, it fails QA.

  Global elements on every page

       Header: logo (links to /), click-to-call phone number as text, and a "
       [PRIMARY_ACTION]" button linking to /contact/. Emergency niches: the header
       button is "Call [CV: phone]" and the quote link sits in the nav.
       Breadcrumbs: none on the homepage. Every other page shows a visible Rank Math trail
       starting with "Home".
       Mobile sticky bar: Call and [PRIMARY_ACTION]. Emergency niches: Call first.
       No form on the homepage. The form lives on /contact/ only (design brief decision:
       cleaner on mobile).

  Business-type layer and niche swap-outs
  The sections below are written once. What changes per niche is decided here, by
  business type, so a dog trainer's page is built from the same template as a roofer's but
  never reads like one. Every niche in the Niche Library maps to one type, plus an optional
  emergency overlay. The copy bot reads the type from [CV: business_type] and the niche's
  Calibration Pack supplies the specifics.

  What every Calibration Pack must now carry for the homepage

  The Landscaper pack covers category blocks and the inner pages. For the homepage to
  build without guesswork, each pack adds a homepage block with:

       The business type and A2 or emergency marker.
       The trust line order and the logo tiers for that niche, with the real body names.
       The four fears for section 5 with two example H3s each.
       The three process steps, in both the quote and the emergency variant where relevant.
       Four niche FAQ slots (FAQ A, FAQ B and the two fallbacks, FAQ C and FAQ D),
       reworded from People Also Ask.
       The local condition anchor list from the table above, as the brief for the geo research.
       One full worked homepage section 3 and section 5 at the required length, marked do
       not reuse.

  Until a niche has its pack, that niche is not built. Packs are the gate.

  Placeholders, claim rules and empty-value fallbacks
  All placeholders come from the Placeholder Glossary (Tab 9). The ones this page uses,
  plus the new definitions v1 was missing:

       [KW] / [KW_PHRASE]: from the Page Map "H1 (Ahrefs)" cell for the homepage row, e.g.
       Roofer Bristol. One unbroken phrase in the H1 and SEO title. In body copy it may take
       joining words so it reads as a sentence ("roofer in Bristol" for Roofer Bristol), with the
       same words in the same order.
       [CATEGORY]: the primary GBP category, e.g. Roofer.
       [TRADE] / [TRADE_LOWER]: the activity form, e.g. Roofing / roofing.
       [TRADE_PERSON] / [TRADE_PERSON_PLURAL]: the person form from the Niche
       Library, e.g. roofer / roofers. Used wherever a heading talks about people, because a
       GBP category isn't always a person ("Lawn Care Service").

       [CUSTOMER_TYPE]: homeowners / dog owners / local businesses, from the Niche
       Library.
       [PROJECT_WORD]: garden / home / roof / dog.
       [GBP_CAT_1…n], [GBP_SVC_1…n], [GBP_AREA_1…n]: exactly as spelt on the GBP.
       [GBP_CAT_n_DISPLAY] is the readable heading form of a GBP category, held in the
       Niche Library (Roofing contractor becomes Roofing, Gutter cleaning service becomes
       Gutter Cleaning). It is used in headings only.
       [CITY]: the primary city, as spelt on the GBP address or service area.
       [AREA_WIDE]: replaces v1's [COUNTY]. The wider area the client actually covers,
       taken from geo research, e.g. North Somerset, North East Lincolnshire, the Humber
       area. Truth check: it must be an area the GBP service areas substantially cover. "Bristol
       & Somerset" fails (Bristol isn't in Somerset); "Grimsby & Lincolnshire" fails for a client
       covering one corner of it. If no honest wider area exists, [AREA_WIDE] is blank and
       headings use "[CITY] and the Surrounding Areas".
       [H1_MODIFIER]: now used only in the SEO title, never in the H1 or the trust line.
       [META_PROOF], [QUOTE_FREE] / [QUOTE_FREE_LOWER]: as in v1, below.
       Niche slots: [NICHE_SLOT: steps], [NICHE_SLOT: FAQ A], [NICHE_SLOT: FAQ B], and
       the fallbacks [NICHE_SLOT: FAQ C] and [NICHE_SLOT: FAQ D]. Written per niche in the
       Niche Library, always at the same length. The number of niches is whatever the Niche
       Library holds; the onboarding form caps at 26.

  Quote and availability claims

       [QUOTE_FREE] = "Free" only if [CV: free_quotes] is yes, meaning every quote is free
       with no callout, survey or visit fee. Otherwise blank, and the copy bot removes the
       word and the space after it.
       "Same Day" only if [CV: same_day_available] is yes. "24/7" only if [CV:
       out_of_hours_available] is "24/7".
       Any sentence about how quotes work uses only [CV: quote_method]. "Fixed price"
       only if confirmed.

  Claim words (UK ASA)

  "best", "#1", "leading", "trusted", "expert", "specialist", "reliable" and "fully insured" need
  proof on file. Use "local" instead. Two scoped exceptions: the section 2 label "Trusted &
  Rated By" is allowed because every logo under it links to the register that proves it, and
  "insured up to £[CV: insurance_cover]" is allowed when the current certificate is on file.
  "Fully insured" with no figure is not.
