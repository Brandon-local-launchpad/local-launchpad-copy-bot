From mowing and edging to hedges, beds and boundaries, this is the day to day work that keeps a garden under control. It suits homeowners who'd rather have lawns, borders and fences looked after than spend every weekend doing it.

### [Lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing)

Grass that gets away from you is harder to cut evenly, and the clippings lie in clumps that shade the lawn underneath. Our [lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing) work sets the height to suit the growth, never takes more than a third off in one cut, then cuts the edges and clears the paths.

### [Fencing](/services/fencing)

Is the fence leaning, rattling or going soft at the bottom? On a [fencing](/services/fencing) visit, loose panels and boards are refixed and a failed post is swapped for a new one. A gate can be hung to close and latch properly.

### [Lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding)

Booking [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) covers thin patches and bare ground, with seed sown over raked soil and pressed in so it makes good contact. Warmth and moisture in the soil decide how quickly it comes up. A lawn that's thick enough to walk on takes longer than the first green.

### [Hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming)

A hedge usually gets booked when it starts blocking the path or the top has grown out of shape. Our [hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming) rounds cut the sides, then the top, to a straight face. That's done with hand shears or a powered trimmer, and the cuttings are cleared off the beds.

### [Mulching](/services/mulching)

Mulch piled against the trunk of a shrub can rot it, and a thin dusting does little for moisture or weeds. A [mulching](/services/mulching) job starts with the beds being weeded. Then they get a layer of bark or compost deep enough to shade the soil, kept clear of the stems.

### [Weed control](/services/weed-control)

Clear beds and paving joints with no green in them are the result most people want. The [weed control](/services/weed-control) we do starts by identifying the weed, since the right method depends on which one it is. Then comes hand weeding, digging or hoeing, with a check for regrowth afterwards.

## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss at the end of a wet winter is normal, and it thins as spring growth picks up. If it holds on through summer, or bare patches never fill, that usually points to shade and damp ground. Scarifying lifts the moss, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) fills thin patches in spring or early autumn while the grass can recover.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge gone wide at the top and thin at the base has usually missed its cuts, so the growth has turned woody. Cutting before midsummer, while the growth is still soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood, though the window varies by species. Bringing one back takes cuts in stages over more than one season.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that reappear through paving joints and across the beds have usually been cut off at the surface. The root stays in the ground and shoots again. Hoeing and lifting them while they're small in spring, before they set seed and the roots go deep, gets the whole plant out. Nothing keeps weeds away for good.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves coming down in autumn is the season, not a fault. The harm depends on how thick they lie, how long they stay and how wet it gets. A damp layer keeps light and air off the grass, and the lawn can turn pale. Clearing them as they fall, before they mat down, lets the grass see daylight.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

The size of the garden moves the price more than anything else, because a bigger lawn, longer hedge and more beds all mean more work. Two gardens on the same street can price quite differently for that reason alone.

### How often we visit

On a regular round each visit is shorter, because less has built up since the last one. A shorter visit is a smaller job to price than a big tidy up of a garden left to grow.

### The size of the garden

A bigger garden means more lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim, so there's simply more to do. Compact gardens and large ones are quite different jobs.

### What each visit covers

The smallest visit is a lawn cut and nothing else. Once the edges, the beds and the hedges are included, there's more work in each visit, and the price reflects that.

### Green waste

How much waste a visit produces, and what has to happen to it, moves the price. Bagging, loading and carrying away hedge cuttings and weeds is extra work. A garden with heavy growth therefore costs more to clear than one with little to dispose of.

## Gardening Questions Answered

### How much does gardening cost in Southampton?

It depends mostly on how big the garden is. A small garden with a lawn and a hedge is priced very differently from a large one. ### Are you insured?

Yes, we're insured up to £5m. The cover is there for the gardening work we do at customers' homes, such as cutting lawns and trimming hedges. That's worth checking with any gardener before the work starts.

### What does a gardening visit include?

Mostly the lawn, the edges, the beds and the hedges. A visit means mowing and edging, weeding the borders and trimming the hedges. Fencing, mulching and lawn seeding are separate jobs. If a tidy lawn is all that's wanted, [lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing) on its own is also an option.

### How do regular visits work?

The schedule is agreed with you. Once the first visit is booked, the visits after it are settled together. The easiest moment to raise it is when you send your enquiry, so mention it then.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, and the work changes with the season. Through the growing season, it's mostly cutting and edging, with hedges trimmed and weeds kept down before they get ahead. As the grass slows in the cold months, the work turns to clearing, cutting back and pruning, and tidying up the beds. Hedge and bed work carries on when the grass doesn't.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

When the soil warms in spring and the lawn starts to grow properly. That first cut should be a light one, taking only the top off, because a hard cut on grass just out of winter stresses it. After that the height can come down gradually, never taking more than about a third off in one cut.
