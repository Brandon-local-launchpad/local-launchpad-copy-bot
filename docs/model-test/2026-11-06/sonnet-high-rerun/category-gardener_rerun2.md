## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss after a wet winter is normal and thins as the grass starts growing in spring. If it holds on through summer, or bare patches never fill, that points to shade and ground that stays soggy after rain. Scarifying lifts the moss, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) on the thin patches in spring or early autumn lets the grass recover.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge wide at the top and thin at the base has often missed its cuts or had them too late, so it went woody. For most hedges, a cut before midsummer, while growth is soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood. Bringing one back means cutting in stages over more than one season, never one hard cut.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that keep reappearing in paving joints or across a bed have usually been cut off at the surface. The root stayed in the ground, so the plant regrows. Hoeing and lifting them while they're small in spring reaches the root as well as the leaf. That's before they set seed and the roots go deep.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves coming down in autumn is the season, not a fault. The trouble starts when they lie thick on the lawn and shut light and air off the grass underneath. How pale it looks depends on how deep the layer is and how wet the autumn is. Clearing through autumn, before they mat down, keeps the grass growing.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

The amount of garden to look after moves the price more than anything else on a gardening job. A quote puts a figure on your own garden once it's clear which jobs you want done.

### How often we visit

The more often a garden is looked after, the less growth builds up between visits, so each visit is shorter. A garden left for a long stretch takes more to bring back under control, and that moves the price.

### The size of the garden

The bigger the garden, the longer each visit takes, and the price follows. Grass covers a wider area to mow and edge, beds take longer to weed and hedges run further. Small front gardens are a much shorter job.

### What each visit covers

A cut on its own is less work than a visit that also edges the lawn, weeds the beds and trims the hedges. The final price follows what each visit includes.

### Green waste

Clippings, hedge trimmings and weeds have to go somewhere, and what happens to them moves the price. A tall hedge or a bed that's been left a long time leaves a lot more to deal with than a routine cut.

## Gardening Questions Answered

### How much does gardening cost in Southampton?

It depends mostly on how big the garden is. A larger garden takes more work on every visit, and that's what moves the figure most. To get a price for yours, ask for a free quote. Sending a few pictures of the beds and lawn helps make the size clear.

### Are you insured?

Yes, we're insured up to £5m. That cover is there in case something goes wrong while the work is being done in your garden, whether that's hedge cutting or a fence going in. It's worth asking any gardener about their cover before work begins.

### What does a gardening visit include?

It covers the lawn, the edges, the beds and the hedges. That means cutting and edging the lawn, weeding the beds, trimming the hedges and spreading mulch where the soil is bare. A [fencing](/services/fencing) job or a reseed can be added as work of its own. Which of those a visit includes depends on the garden and the jobs you ask for.

### How do regular visits work?

The schedule is agreed with you. What a regular round includes depends on what the garden has in it. The jobs are settled with you as well, whether that's cutting the lawn, keeping the hedges in line or weeding the beds.

### Do you garden all year round?

Gardens need attention in every season, though the jobs change. While the grass is growing, the work is mostly mowing, neatening the lawn edges and keeping weeds down. In the cold months it turns to pruning and cutting back, and mowing pauses in a drought or on very wet or frozen ground.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

Once the grass starts growing again as the soil warms in spring. Keep that first cut light, taking just the top off and never more than a third of the height. A hard cut stresses grass that's only just getting going. The height can come down over the next few cuts as growth picks up.
