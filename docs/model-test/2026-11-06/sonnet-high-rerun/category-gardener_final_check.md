This covers the everyday jobs in a home garden: the lawn, the hedges, the borders and the fence line, whether the garden is at the front or the back. It's for homeowners anywhere in our service area who'd rather have someone else do the cutting, weeding and mulching.

### [Lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing)

When the grass has got long and the edges are spilling over the path, the whole garden looks behind. Our [lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing) work sets the cutting height for the grass as it is. It then edges the lawn along the borders and sweeps up the cuttings.

### [Fencing](/services/fencing)

Is a panel rattling, or a post starting to lean? On a [fencing](/services/fencing) visit the failing section is taken out and new posts are set and lined up. The rails or panels are then fixed to them, with a gate hung where the run needs one.

### [Lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding)

Overseeding puts new grass into thin and worn patches where the old has given up. Booking [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) means the seed is broadcast, raked in and firmed, then watered. How quickly it comes up depends on the weather, soil warmth and moisture, and a full lawn takes longer.

### [Hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming)

Hedges usually get booked in when they start narrowing the path or the top goes uneven. Our [hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming) rounds cut the sides first, then the top, back to a clean line. Hand shears or a powered trimmer do the work, and the ground is left clear.

### [Mulching](/services/mulching)

Mulch heaped against stems traps damp at the bark, and a thin dusting lets weeds straight through. A [mulching](/services/mulching) job spreads it around the planting instead and keeps it off the base of each plant. It goes on thick enough to cover bare soil.

### [Weed control](/services/weed-control)

Paving joints and beds free of weeds are the result most people want from [weed control](/services/weed-control). The work starts with working out what's growing, since that decides the method. It might be hand weeding, hoeing or digging out roots, with the plants worth keeping protected throughout.

## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss at the end of a wet winter is normal, and it thins as the grass starts growing in spring. Where it holds on through summer, or bare patches never fill, shade and damp ground are the cause. Scarifying lifts the moss, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) fills thin patches in spring or early autumn, while the grass can recover.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge gone wide at the top and thin at the base usually had cuts missed or made at the wrong time. The growth then went woody. Cutting before midsummer, while growth is soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood, though species differ. Bringing one back takes staged cuts over more than a season.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that reappear through paving joints and across beds are usually regrowing from roots left in the ground. Cutting or pulling off the top leaves the root to send new shoots. Digging or lifting the root is what stops that. Spring is easiest, hoeing while weeds are small, before they set seed and the roots go deep.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves coming down in autumn is the season, not a fault. Where they stay on the grass, they shut out light and air. How pale the lawn gets depends on how thick the leaf fall is and how wet the autumn turns out. Clearing them through autumn, before they mat down, lets the grass breathe.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

The size of the garden does more to move the price than anything else on a gardening job. A quote gives you a price for your own garden, so you aren't left guessing from a general rate.

### How often we visit

A regular round keeps each visit shorter, because the garden doesn't get far ahead of the work. That means the price per visit is lower than for an occasional big tidy up.

### The size of the garden

A larger garden means more lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim. Each one is more work in a visit. Small front gardens and long back gardens are priced differently for that reason alone.

### What each visit covers

A cut on its own is a lighter visit than one covering the lawn, edges, beds and hedges, and the price follows what's included. Adding weeding or a hedge trim to a cut adds to the work, and so to the price.

### Green waste

Hedge trimmings, cuttings and cleared leaves add up to a lot of waste. Whether it leaves the garden or stays in it moves the price. Taking it away means loading, carrying and disposing of it.

### What does a gardening visit include?

A visit can cover the lawn, the edges, the beds and the hedges. That means cutting and edging the grass, weeding and mulching the borders, and trimming hedges back to their line. Which jobs go into a visit depends on the garden, and [weed control](/services/weed-control) comes in where the beds have filled with weeds.

### How do regular visits work?

The schedule is agreed with you, rather than set for you. How often we call is something we'll talk through once we've seen the details and what you want kept under control. Each visit then works through the jobs that garden has, such as cutting, edging, weeding and hedges.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, there's gardening work in every season, and the jobs change with it. Through the growing season it's mostly cutting the lawn, edging and keeping beds clear of weeds. When it turns cold, the work moves to clearing up, cutting back and pruning, because the grass has stopped growing and the plants are resting.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

When the grass starts growing again as the soil warms in spring. Cutting before then does little, because there's nothing to cut. The first cut should be a light one, taking just the top off, because grass cut hard after winter is put under stress. Each cut after that can take a little more, never more than about a third of the growth at once.
