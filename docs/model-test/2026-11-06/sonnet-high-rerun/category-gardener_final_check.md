A hedge starts to matter the moment it blocks the path or the light from a window. Our [hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming) rounds cut the sides first, then the top, back to a clean line with shears or a powered trimmer. The cuttings are swept up afterwards.

Mulch piled against shrub stems can damage them, and a thin dusting does little to stop weeds coming through. A [mulching](/services/mulching) job starts by weeding the bed. Bark or compost then goes down thickly enough to cover the soil, kept clear of the stems.

## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss at the end of a wet winter is normal and thins as the grass starts growing in spring. Where it holds through summer, or bare patches never fill, shade and slow draining damp ground are the cause. Scarifying lifts the moss out, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) fills thin patches in spring or early autumn, while the grass can recover.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge gone wide at the top and thin at the base has usually missed cuts or had them too late. The growth then turned woody. Cutting before midsummer, while growth is still soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood; timing varies by species. Bringing one back takes cuts over more than one season, after a nest check.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that return through paving joints and across the beds have usually had the top snapped off. The root stayed in the ground, so it simply grows back. Hoeing or lifting them in spring, while they're small, gets the root out before they set seed and before the roots go deep.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves falling in autumn is just the season, not a fault. Where they lie thick on the grass and stay put through wet weather, they keep light and air off it. The lawn underneath turns pale and thin. Clearing them through autumn, before they mat down in the wet, lets the grass keep getting both.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

How much garden there is to look after moves the price more than anything else. A small front lawn and a long back garden with borders and hedges make for very different visits. The price for either is agreed with you before any work starts.

### How often we visit

A garden visited regularly has less to catch up on, so each visit is shorter and the price for each one comes down with it. Long gaps between visits mean more growth to cut back each time.

### The size of the garden

A bigger garden means more lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim. Each of those adds to the work in a visit. At the other end, a small front garden takes less.

### What each visit covers

A lawn cut on its own is a smaller job than a visit that also takes in the edges, the beds and the hedges. The more of those you want done in a visit, the more work there is to price.

### Green waste

Hedge cuttings, clippings and pulled weeds all have to go somewhere, and what happens to them changes how much work a visit involves. A garden with a lot of green waste to deal with costs more than one with very little.

## Gardening Questions Answered

### How much does gardening cost in Southampton?

It depends mostly on how much ground the garden covers. There's no standard rate, so the figure comes from a free quote for the work you want done. Sending a few photos with your request helps us give you a figure.

### Are you insured?

Yes, we're insured up to £5m. That cover is there for accidents or damage connected to the work we carry out in a garden. It applies whether that's a lawn visit, a hedge cut or a fence repair.

### What does a gardening visit include?

A visit can cover the lawn, its edges, the borders and the hedges. That means mowing and trimming the edges, [weed control](/services/weed-control) in the borders, and cutting the hedges back to their line. Mulching, lawn seeding and fencing are available too, when a garden calls for them. Which of these goes into your visits is agreed with you.

### How do regular visits work?

We talk through how often we come and what suits the garden. If reaching the back garden means a side gate or a narrow path, mention it early, so the first visit runs smoothly.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, there's work for a gardener in every season. While the grass is growing, that's mostly cutting the lawn and keeping the hedges and beds in check. In the colder months the work turns to clearing, cutting back and tidying the beds. The lawn is left uncut when the ground is very wet or frozen.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

Once the grass starts growing again in spring as the soil warms up. The first cut should be a light one, taking just the top off rather than cutting hard. Grass that's been resting through winter is easily stressed. Taking no more than about a third off the height in one cut is a good rule.
