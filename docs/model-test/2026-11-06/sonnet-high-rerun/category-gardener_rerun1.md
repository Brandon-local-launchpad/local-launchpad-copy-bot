### [Lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing)

Long grass that's been left to get away is hard to cut cleanly, and the cuttings clump and shade the lawn underneath. Our [lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing) work sets the blades to a sensible height for the season, cuts evenly, then edges and strims along the paths and beds.

### [Fencing](/services/fencing)

Is a panel rattling in the wind, or a board working loose? A [fencing](/services/fencing) visit deals with both. That can mean refixing boards, replacing a soft post, or putting in a run of close board or panel, with the old fence cleared first.

### [Lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding)

Booking [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) covers sowing bare patches and overseeding a thin lawn, with the seed raked in and firmed. How fast it comes up depends on the warmth and moisture of the ground, and a thick lawn takes far longer to build. Watering afterwards matters as much as the sowing.

### [Hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming)

Late spring is when many hedges first start to look shaggy, with fresh growth pushing out of the sides and over the top. Our [hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming) rounds shape the sides first, then level the top against a straight line. Thick growth gets a powered trimmer, and lighter growth gets hand shears.

### [Mulching](/services/mulching)

Mulch heaped against the stems of shrubs can damage them, and a thin dusting leaves weeds free to come through. A [mulching](/services/mulching) job spreads bark, compost or woodchip thickly enough to shade the soil, and leaves a clear gap round every stem.

### [Weed control](/services/weed-control)

Clear beds and paving joints with no green lines through them are the result most people are after. The [weed control](/services/weed-control) we do gets there by hand weeding, hoeing and digging. We work carefully around the plants you want to keep, one bed at a time.

## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss after a wet winter is normal and thins once the grass starts growing in spring. What isn't normal is moss that holds on through summer, or bare patches that never fill. Those point to shade and damp ground. Scarifying lifts the moss, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) thickens thin patches in spring or early autumn.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge wide at the top, thin at the base and pushing over the path has usually missed its cuts, so growth has gone woody. Trimming before midsummer, while new growth is soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood, and the window varies by species. Bringing one back means cutting in stages over more than one season.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that keep reappearing in paving joints and across beds usually still have the root in the ground, so the plant regrows. Lifting the whole root is the fix, not cutting the top off. In spring, hoeing and lifting while weeds are small gets them out before they set seed and the roots go deep.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves falling in autumn is the season, not a fault, and a scattering on the lawn does no harm. Trouble starts where they lie thick, because they keep light and air off the grass, and wet weather packs them down. Clearing them through autumn, before they mat in the wet, lets the grass underneath keep growing.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

How much garden there is to keep in order moves the price more than anything else. Once we know what the garden involves, we'll give you a price for the work you want done.

### How often we visit

How often a garden is visited changes the price of each visit. A regular round keeps every visit shorter, because less has grown between them. Each one costs less than a single big tidy up after a long gap.

### The size of the garden

A bigger garden means more lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim. The price rises with the amount there is to do. Small front gardens are lighter work than back gardens with deep borders.

### What each visit covers

A cut on its own is a shorter, cheaper visit than one that covers the lawn, the edges, the beds and the hedges. Whatever's in the visit is what the price pays for.

### Green waste

Hedge trimmings, mown clippings and pulled weeds add up to more than most people expect. Loading, carrying and disposing of that material takes time. What happens to it after a visit is one of the things that moves the price.

### What does a gardening visit include?

It takes in mowing, edging, weeding, mulching and hedge work. The grass is cut and edged, the borders weeded and mulched, and the hedges trimmed back to their line. Jobs like [fencing](/services/fencing) repairs or sowing bare patches are things we do as well, and we'll say what each involves when you ask.

### How do regular visits work?

The schedule is agreed with you. A regular round is a set of ongoing garden jobs, such as mowing, edging and hedge work, carried on through the year. Tell us which of those you'd like looked after and the pattern gets settled from there, so the round fits the garden you actually have.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, there's something to do in every season. Through the growing months the work is cutting and edging the lawn and keeping beds weeded. In the cold months it turns to clearing and pruning. Wet or frozen ground slows some jobs, like cutting a lawn, so they wait for better conditions.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

It should happen once the grass starts growing again in spring. That follows the soil warming up. Keep the first cut light, taking just the top off, because cutting hard after winter stresses the grass and can leave it thin or patchy. Later cuts can come down gradually as growth picks up.
