Hedges need trimming most in early summer, when the new growth has filled out past the line. Our [hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming) rounds keep the sides square and the top level, so the lines stay tidy along the front of the house between visits.

Bark or chippings piled against a stem can rot the plant at its base. A thin scatter does little to hold the soil's moisture. The [mulching](/services/mulching) we put down goes on evenly, kept clear of every stem. Through a dry spell the soil stays damp, and weed seedlings get little light.

## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss in a lawn after a wet winter is normal, and it thins as the grass grows in spring. Summer moss or bare patches that never fill is not. Shade and damp ground are the usual cause. Scarify to lift the moss, then overseed thin areas in spring or early autumn through our [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) work.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge gone wide at the top and bare at the base usually means cuts were missed or made at the wrong time. Cutting before midsummer, while new growth is soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood, and the window varies by species. Bringing it back takes cuts over several seasons, never one hard cut.

### Weeds that come straight back

A weed that keeps returning usually has some root left in the soil, and a new shoot grows from it. Lifting the whole weed out while it's small, in spring, stops that. Before it sets seed and before the root runs deep, it comes out cleanly. How soon it returns depends on how much root stayed behind.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves falling in autumn is the season, not a fault. Trouble starts when they stay on the grass. The longer they lie, and the deeper and wetter the pile, the more light and air is cut off. Raking or blowing them off before they mat down in wet weather stops the grass being smothered.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

The biggest thing that moves the price of gardening is how much lawn, border and hedge there is to keep up on each visit. Knowing that before anything else tells you what your garden is likely to need.

### How often we visit

A regular round means each visit is shorter, because it picks up where the last one left the lawn and beds. So the price for each visit comes in below what an occasional big tidy up costs.

### The size of the garden

More lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim each take time. The size of the garden is what changes most from one job to the next, so it's the first thing to size up.

### What each visit covers

Cutting the grass alone leaves the edges and the borders as they were. A round covers those too. Paths get edged, borders that have crept over the lawn get pulled back, and overgrown hedges are trimmed to their line.

### Green waste

What happens to the cuttings and clippings after a visit moves the price, since taking them away is extra work. Whether they're left on site or taken away is worth settling before the work starts.

### Do you garden all year round?

The work follows the seasons, so a cold spell means different jobs from summer. Mowing belongs to the growing months, roughly March to October, and is paused in a prolonged drought or on frozen ground. Clearing, weeding and pruning fit the cooler months, when plants are dormant and the beds are easier to see.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

When the grass starts growing again in spring, as the soil warms. The first cut of the year is a light one. Only the top is taken off, never the lawn cut down hard, so the grass isn't stressed while it wakes up.
