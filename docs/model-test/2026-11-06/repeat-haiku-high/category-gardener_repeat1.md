## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss in a lawn after a wet winter is normal and thins as spring growth returns. When it stays through summer, or bare patches never fill, shade and damp ground are usually behind it. Scarify out the moss and overseed thin areas in spring or early autumn, while the grass can recover; large bare areas need [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding).

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge left for a season or two grows wide on top and thins at the base, as the growth goes woody. Cutting before midsummer, while new growth is still soft, keeps the blade in green wood, though the window varies by species. Bringing one back takes several cuts over a few seasons, never one hard cut.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that keep coming back have roots left in the ground, and the top regrows from whatever was missed. How fast depends on the weed: a perennial with a deep root or running stem comes back from a small piece. Lifting the whole root breaks the cycle, and it's easiest in early spring, before seed sets.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves dropping in autumn are normal. The damage comes when a thick layer lies on the grass in wet weather. It cuts off light and air, and the patch yellows by midwinter. How soon it happens depends on how deep the leaves lie and how damp the ground stays. Clearing them before they mat down lets the grass breathe.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

How much garden there is to cover moves the price more than anything else, so a figure only means something for your own plot. Asking for one is the way to get a figure for the garden you have.

### How often we visit

In a regular round, each visit has less to do. So the price per visit sits lower than one big tidy up after a garden has been left. Keeping growth short between visits is what makes that possible.

### The size of the garden

More lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim, and each one adds time to every visit. A small garden with one lawn and a short hedge takes far less time than a large one.

### What each visit covers

A cut on its own takes less time than a visit that also covers the edges, the beds and the hedges. Each job added to a visit lengthens it, so the lawn alone is the smallest version of the work.

### Green waste

What happens to the cuttings, prunings and weeds moves the price, since taking them away adds handling and a trip to the tip. Ask how the waste is handled when you enquire, as the answer changes the figure.

## Gardening Questions Answered

### How much does gardening cost in Southampton?

It depends on the work your garden needs. The biggest factor is how much lawn, borders and hedge the plot holds, because that sets the hours on site. Send a few photos and ask for a free quote, and we'll come back with a price for that garden.

### Are you insured?

Yes, we're insured up to £5m. That cover is for the work we do at your property, so it applies to jobs in your own garden. It's there for the work itself, not for how a lawn or plant comes on afterwards.

### What does a gardening visit include?

A visit covers the lawn, the paths, the borders and any hedge due a trim. Most of the time goes on mowing and edging, with weeds in the planting cleared through [weed control](/services/weed-control). Mulching, lawn seeding and fencing are separate jobs, each with its own page.

### How do regular visits work?

Each round is agreed with you, and the schedule is set for your garden. The jobs in it can run from [lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing) through to [weed control](/services/weed-control), and we'll go through which ones you need when you contact us.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, the work runs through the year, though what it involves shifts with the seasons. Through the warmer months the lawn needs cutting most often, and the beds need weeding as growth comes through. In the colder months the grass slows right down, so the lawn needs far less, and the time moves to clearing and tidying the planting.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

Once the grass starts growing again as the soil warms in spring. The first cut is a light one, with only the top taken off, because cutting hard after winter stresses grass that's still waking up.
