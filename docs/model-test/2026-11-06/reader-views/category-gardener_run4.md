## Our Gardener Services in Southampton

Gardener work is the regular upkeep that keeps a garden looking cared for: mowing, edging, hedges, beds and the weeds that creep into them. It suits homeowners across the whole area we cover, from a small front garden to a large back lawn with borders.

### [Lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing)

When the grass has grown over the edge of the path, a quick trim won't do it. Our [lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing) work sets the blades to a height the grass can take through summer. Then we edge along the paving and beds so the lines stay sharp.

### [Fencing](/services/fencing)

Does a leaning fence need replacing? Often the posts are the problem, not the panels. A [fencing](/services/fencing) job starts with the posts, set firmly in concrete so the wood sits away from wet ground. The rails and panels go on after that, and the gate is hung to match.

### [Lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding)

Seeding a thin patch starts with taking out the dead material and firming the soil, because new grass can't root through a layer of it. Booking [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) covers that prep, then the seed goes down and the patch is left to take.

### [Hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming)

Come late spring, hedges start pushing over the path and onto the pavement, and that's when most people go looking for help. A [hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming) round brings the sides and top back to a clean line, and tidies the edges where the cut meets the path.

### [Mulching](/services/mulching)

Mulch laid as a dusting, or heaped against the stems of a shrub, does little good and can rot the stem. Instead, a [mulching](/services/mulching) job puts the layer on thick enough to hold moisture and keeps it clear of the stems, so the soil stays damp for longer.

### [Weed control](/services/weed-control)

Paving joints and bed edges that stay clear is the result most people want. The [weed control](/services/weed-control) work starts by identifying each weed, then clears it by the method that suits it. Plants around it are protected, and regrowth needs a second look, because a weed cleared once often returns.

## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

A mossy lawn after a wet winter is usual and thins as the grass grows in spring. Moss that holds on through summer, or bare patches that never fill, points to shade and ground that stays wet. Scarifying lifts the moss, and [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) fills the gaps in spring or early autumn. Mowing alone won't clear it.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge wide at the top and bare at the base has usually been cut too late or too seldom. Cutting before midsummer, while the new growth is soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood, and the window varies by species. Bringing it back takes cuts over more than one season, never one hard cut.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds return after clearing when some of the root stays in the ground. A root or a broken piece of stem can regrow, and a long taproot gets going again much sooner than a shallow one. Hoeing and lifting weeds in spring, while they're small and before they set seed, leaves less behind to come back.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves falling through autumn are normal. How much harm they do depends on how deep they lie and how wet the weather stays. A thick layer under overhanging trees, soaked by rain, starves the grass of light and air until it yellows. Clearing them while they're still loose, before they mat down, keeps the colour in the lawn.

A regular round keeps each visit short, because the grass and beds never get left long enough to need a full clear up. So the price of each visit sits lower than an occasional big tidy after a long gap.

A bigger garden means more to do on every visit. Extra lawn means more edging, extra bed means more weeding, and a longer hedge means another line to hold. A small front strip with one bed is a quick visit.

Mowing alone leaves the edges, beds and hedges to be sorted another day, and they soon show it. A full visit covers all four in one go, so nothing is left to catch up on.

### Green waste

Whether the cuttings and clippings leave the garden moves the price, because taking them away is extra work on top of the cut. Say at the start if you want them removed, so the quote covers what you actually need.

### How do regular visits work?

The schedule is agreed with you. A regular round is the jobs a garden needs each time, such as cutting and edging the lawn, weeding the beds and trimming any hedges. What a visit takes in depends on what has grown since the last one.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, the work changes with the seasons rather than stopping. Through the growing season the grass needs cutting, edges need keeping sharp and the beds need weeding. In the colder months the work shifts to pruning and tidying, since the growth has stopped. Cutting stops while the ground is frozen or sodden, and starts again once the grass picks up.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

Once the grass starts growing as the soil warms in spring. The first cut is a light one, taking just the top off, so the grass isn't stressed after winter. Cutting too low at that point stresses the grass and leaves it slow to green up.
