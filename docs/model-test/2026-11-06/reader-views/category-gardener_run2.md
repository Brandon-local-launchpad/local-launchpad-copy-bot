## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

A wet winter leaves most lawns mossy, and it thins out once the grass starts growing again. What isn't normal is a lawn that stays mossy through summer, or bare patches that never fill, which point to shade and damp ground. Scarifying lifts the moss, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) in spring or early autumn fills the thin areas.

### Hedges that have got away

A hedge that's wide at the top and thin at the base has usually missed its cuts, or been cut too late, so the growth has gone woody. It often leans over the path too. Cutting before midsummer keeps the blade in soft growth. Bringing one back means working in stages over more than one season.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that come back through the paving joints and across the beds have usually had their roots left in the ground, so the top regrows. How quickly depends on the weed, the weather and how deep the roots had got. Hoeing and lifting in spring, while weeds are small, gets them before they set seed.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves falling in autumn is the season, not a fault. The trouble comes when they lie on the grass and mat down, keeping light and air off it, so the lawn goes pale underneath. How badly depends on how many trees overhang and how wet the autumn is. Clearing through autumn lets the grass breathe.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

How big the garden is moves the price more than anything else for this work. Every quote is free, with no obligation, so you can see the figure before anything starts.

### How often we visit

A regular round means each visit is shorter. That brings the price per visit down, compared with an occasional big tidy up of a garden that has been left to grow.

### The size of the garden

More lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim all add time to a visit. Small front gardens take far less of it than long back ones, where the boundary hedge alone can run the full length.

### What each visit covers

A cut on its own costs less than a visit that covers the lawn, the edges, the beds and the hedges. The more jobs in a visit, the longer it takes.

### Green waste

What happens to the clippings, hedge trimmings and pulled weeds moves the price. Bagging up and carrying away a hedge's worth of cuttings is extra work and extra time. A visit where little is left to clear is quicker.

### What does a gardening visit include?

A visit covers whichever of the garden's regular jobs it needs. That can mean mowing and edging the grass, weeding the borders, mulching bare soil and trimming hedges. Fencing and lawn seeding are separate jobs, booked on their own. Our [lawn mowing](/services/lawn-mowing) page explains what a cut involves.

### How do regular visits work?

The schedule is agreed with you. We talk through the garden, what needs doing and what suits you, then set the visits up around that. Nothing is fixed until you're happy with it, and you can ask us questions about it first.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, the work follows the garden through the year. Through the growing season it's mostly cutting, edging and weeding. In the colder months it moves to pruning and tidying the beds, while the grass rests. Frozen or waterlogged ground is left alone, since working it does more harm than good.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

The first cut comes in spring, when the soil warms and the lawn picks up. It should be a light one, just the top taken off. Cutting hard on a first visit stresses grass that's only just recovering and can leave it patchy. The height comes down gradually over the next few cuts.
