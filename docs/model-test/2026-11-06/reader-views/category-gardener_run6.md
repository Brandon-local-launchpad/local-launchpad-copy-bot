## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

A wet winter leaves most lawns mossy, and the moss thins as spring growth starts. What isn't normal is moss that holds on through summer, or bare patches that never fill, which point to shade and ground that stays damp. Scarifying lifts the moss out, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) goes into the thin patches in spring or early autumn.

### Hedges that have got away

Wide at the top, thin at the base, spilling over the path: that's a hedge whose cuts were missed or made late. The growth has gone woody. A cut before midsummer, while the season's growth is soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood. Bringing one back takes stages over more than one season, never one hard cut.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that keep coming back through paving joints and beds have usually been cut off at the top, leaving the root in the ground. Hoeing and lifting them while they're small in spring, before they set seed and the roots go deep, is far easier than clearing them later.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

block4 card_body: Leaves coming down in autumn is the season, not a fault. Where they lie thick on the grass, they keep light and air off it, so the lawn underneath turns pale. Wet weather packs them down flat. Clearing them through autumn, while they're still loose, lets light and air back to the grass.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

The size of the garden moves the price more than anything else, because a bigger garden simply means more work in every visit. Ask for a quote and you'll have a figure for your own garden.

### How often we visit

A regular round keeps each visit short, because the garden never gets far ahead of the work. That makes the price per visit lower than for an occasional big tidy up of the same garden.

### The size of the garden

A bigger garden means more lawn to cut and edge, more borders to weed and more hedge to trim. The extra time adds up, so a large garden costs more to look after than a small one.

### What each visit covers

A cut on its own costs less than a visit that covers the lawn, the edges, the beds and the hedges. Seeding the thin patches or spreading mulch are extra jobs on top, and each one adds to the price.

### Green waste

Hedge trimmings, mown clippings and weeds pulled from the beds all pile up, and where they end up moves the price. Bagging, loading and carrying them off takes time and a disposal point; material left in the garden takes none.

## Gardening Questions Answered

### How much does gardening cost in Southampton?

It depends mostly on how big the garden is. Quotes are free, so the easiest way to get a figure is to ask for one. Send a few photos of the lawn, beds and hedges, and the price you're given reflects what's actually there rather than an average.

### Are you insured?

Yes, we're insured up to £5m. Garden work brings mowers, powered hedge trimmers and ladders close to houses, windows, fences and parked cars. That's the kind of risk insurance exists for. Fencing adds posts, rails and panels being lifted and set near boundaries.

### What does a gardening visit include?

It's the jobs you choose: mowing, edging, weeding and hedge cutting. The grass is cut, the borders are weeded and overgrown growth is brought back to its line. Fencing, lawn seeding and mulching are separate jobs, and the [weed control](/services/weed-control) page explains how roots and seedlings are dealt with.

### How do regular visits work?

The schedule is agreed with you. Each visit takes in the jobs you've asked for, such as mowing and edging the lawn, weeding the beds or cutting back a hedge. If the garden is reached through a locked gate or the house, mention it when you enquire, as that affects how the visit is arranged.

### Do you garden all year round?

Yes, the work changes with the season. Through the growing months the grass needs regular cutting, beds need weeding and hedges need keeping to their line. In the colder months the work moves to cutting back borders and pruning while growth slows. Frozen or sodden grass is better left alone, since walking on it damages it.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

When the grass starts growing again as the soil warms in spring. Make that first cut a light one, taking off just the top. Cutting hard after winter stresses grass that's only just waking up. Bring the height down gradually as growth picks up, never removing more than a third in one cut.
