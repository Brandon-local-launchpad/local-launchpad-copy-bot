## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

At the end of a wet winter, moss in the lawn is normal and thins as the grass grows in spring. Moss through summer, or patches that never fill, is not. Shade and damp ground are the usual cause. The fix is to scarify and overseed the thin patches in spring or early autumn, through our [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) work.

### Hedges that have got away

RETURN: card_body: A hedge left unclipped grows out at the top and thins at the base, since old wood rarely sprouts again. Late cuts land in that old wood. A cut made while new growth is soft keeps the blade out, though the window varies by species. Recovery takes several cuts over more than one season, never one hard cut.

### Weeds that come straight back

RETURN: card_body: Weeds that return through paving joints or across a bed have usually left a piece of root behind. How soon they come back depends on how much root was left and how deep it ran. Lifting the whole plant out, root and all, while the weeds are small in spring, before they set seed, is what breaks the cycle.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves falling in autumn are the season, not a fault. The trouble starts when they stay on the grass. A thick layer blocks light and air, and the longer it lies, the paler the lawn gets. Clearing them through autumn, before they mat down in wet weather, keeps the grass in the light it needs for winter.
