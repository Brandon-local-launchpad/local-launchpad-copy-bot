Lawn seeding starts with clearing the old growth, then levelling and firming a seedbed before the seed is raked in and watered. Booking [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) is how thin or bare ground gets a new covering of grass. Patches that were only worn, not shaded, usually take well to it.

Hedges tend to get a cut when they start crowding the path or hiding the gate, and that's when the [hedge trimming](/services/hedge-trimming) comes in. The sides are shaped first, then the top, keeping to the line the hedge already has, with hand shears or a powered trimmer.

## Common Gardening Problems

### A lawn full of moss and bare patches

Moss in a lawn at the end of a wet winter is normal, and it thins once the grass starts growing again in spring. If it holds on through summer, or bare patches never fill, shade and damp ground are usually behind it. Scarifying lifts the moss, then [lawn seeding](/services/lawn-seeding) fills the thin areas in spring or early autumn.

### Hedges that have got away

Wide at the top, thin at the base, growth over the path: cuts were missed or made too late and the growth went woody. A cut before midsummer, while growth is soft, keeps the blade out of thick wood, though species differ. Bringing one back takes cuts in stages over more than one season, never one hard cut.

### Weeds that come straight back

Weeds that push through paving joints and fill the beds soon after clearing have usually had their roots left in the ground. The roots then send up fresh growth. Spring is the time to hoe and lift them while they're small, before they set seed and the roots go deep.

### Leaves and debris burying the lawn

Leaves falling in autumn is the season, not a fault. The trouble starts when they stay put on the grass, keeping light and air off it, so the lawn is pale underneath by midwinter. Clearing through autumn, before the leaves mat down in the wet, keeps the blades in daylight.

## What Affects the Cost of Gardening in Southampton

The size of the garden moves the price most, because a bigger garden means more to do on every visit. Ask for a quote and you'll get a figure for your own garden rather than a guess.

### How often we visit

A regular round keeps each visit short. That makes the price per visit lower than an occasional big tidy up, when the lawn, beds and hedges have all grown away.

### The size of the garden

More lawn means more to cut and edge, more borders means more weeding, and a longer hedge means more to trim. A small town garden and a big villa garden are very different visits, and the price follows the amount of work.

### What each visit covers

A mow on its own is quicker than a visit that covers the lawn, the edges, the beds and the hedges. What's in the visit changes the price. Mowing, edging, weeding and hedge trimming together take longer than any one of them.

### Green waste

Clippings, hedge trimmings and weeds all have to go somewhere, and whether the waste is taken away or left with you moves the price. It's worth saying which you'd prefer when you ask for a quote.

### Do you garden all year round?

The work changes with the season, but the garden never stops needing something. From spring into autumn the grass is growing, so it's cutting, edging and weeding. In the colder months, hedges and shrubs get pruned and the beds tidied. Mowing eases off once growth stops, and waits on frozen or very wet ground.

### When should the first cut of the year happen?

The first cut comes once the soil warms and growth picks up after winter. Take only the top off that first time, because cutting hard on grass that's only just stirring stresses it. The height can then come down by degrees as the lawn thickens and the cuts become more regular.
