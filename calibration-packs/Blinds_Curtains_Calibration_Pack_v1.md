## 1. The trade and its reader

Most people who read a blinds and curtains site have nothing broken. They want a room darker, cooler, more private or finished. This is a desire led trade, and the copy has to work without inventing problems.

- **The home customer** chooses a look but buys a result: light, privacy, heat, warmth. Their fears are practical: a blind too narrow that lets light down the sides, curtains that hang short, a fitting that pulls out of the wall. Copy for them talks about what the product does in that window, and the measuring and fixing they cannot judge.
- **The awkward window customer** has a bay, roof window, conservatory, tilt and turn window or tall stairwell window that shop bought options don't fit. Copy for them names the window and what it needs.
- **The family customer** wants cords dealt with. Copy for them is calm and accurate about what usually applies.
- **The commercial customer** needs glare control, privacy, durability and fabrics that meet the building's requirements. Copy for them is plain and practical.

The voice is a fitter who measures and fixes for a living, explaining it plainly to a homeowner. Never a showroom selling a lifestyle.

**Desire led, window led and problem led, inside type A.** A type A block 4 card is framed as "signs you need it", but nobody has symptoms of wanting Roman blinds. For most of this trade the "sign" is the customer's situation, not a fault:

- **Desire led** (write the situation): made to measure blinds and curtains, roller, Roman, Venetian, vertical, fitted frame and motorised blinds, curtain tracks and poles, plantation shutters, awnings, commercial blinds.
- **Window led** (a situation the window causes): conservatory blinds, roof window and skylight blinds, bay window curtains and blinds. Write what the glass or shape does to the room, never a fault in the house.
- **Problem led** (write symptom, cause, consequence): blind repairs, curtain alterations and repairs, and child safe blinds where the customer already has corded blinds.

**The hidden layers.** The customer judges fabric and colour. What decides whether it works is what they can't judge: inside or outside the recess, overlap past the frame, stack back (how much window is covered when open), recess depth against handles, and what the bracket is fixed into. A card that names one of these reads as a fitter, not a catalogue.

Not a seasonal niche, but if a card mentions the time of year, give the reason in the sun or the glass (low winter sun, conservatory heat from late spring).

## 3. Local detail that works in this trade

Windows are set by the age and style of the house, so this indoor trade still has real local detail.

**Strong anchors** (area wide and checkable): housing era and the windows it was built with (bays on Victorian, Edwardian and 1930s houses; sash windows with deep recesses on period terraces; 1980s and 1990s homes built or extended with uPVC windows and conservatories; new builds with plasterboard reveals and steel lintels that change how brackets are fixed; tall windows in converted mills or Georgian townhouses); flats where leases ask for a uniform look from outside; terraces on the pavement, where daytime privacy matters; roof windows where the research confirms many loft conversions; commercial mix for commercial blinds.

**Every anchor must change the work.** A 1930s bay is only worth mentioning if it then explains the bent track or a blind per pane. A new build is only worth mentioning if it then explains fixing into plasterboard or a steel lintel.

**Do not:** claim windows in an area are draughty or due for replacement; claim what is in anyone's window today; name a street's orientation the research doesn't support.

## 4. Craft rules for this trade

1. **Write the result, then the product.** "Dark enough to sleep past five in June" before "blackout roller blind".
2. **Name the window, not just the blind.** A bay, a tilt and turn window, a roof window, a sash with a shallow recess. The window decides what will work.
3. **Measuring and fixing are the trade.** One specific about the recess, the overlap, the handles or the brackets is worth more than any line about fabric.
4. **Be accurate about light and heat.** Blackout fabric still lets light round the edges unless the blind is fitted to cut that gap (fitted frame blinds, side channels, overlap past the frame). Thermal linings reduce heat loss through glass; they don't heat a room. Say what each does, not more.
5. **Child safety is stated as what usually applies, calmly.** New blinds with cords or chains are usually supplied and fitted with a safety device, such as a chain tensioner fixed to the wall or a cleat fitted out of a child's reach, and cordless or motorised blinds remove the cord altogether. Older blinds can usually be made safer. Never frighten, never blame parents, never name a standard number.
6. **Never promise what the building or the sun controls.** No room "will stay cool", no blind is "completely blackout", no curtain "cuts heating bills by" any amount. Say what the product reduces or blocks.

## 5. What good looks like: one example per field type

### Homepage service card (type A, Made to measure blinds): 40 to 55 words

```
home_services_cards:
  - card_name: Made to measure blinds | card_text: A blind that's a few millimetres too narrow lets a line of light down each side all night. Good [made to measure blinds](/services/made-to-measure-blinds/) start with how the window is measured: inside the recess or across it, how far past the frame, and room left for the handles to turn.
```

Why it works: opens on a result the reader recognises, then names the measuring layer they can't judge themselves. It describes good practice as a principle, not this business's process, and carries no local detail.

### Category page service card (type A, Conservatory blinds): 40 to 55 words

```
cat_services_cards:
  - card_name: Conservatory blinds | card_text: A conservatory that's too hot to sit in by early afternoon and too cold by October is down to the glass, especially the roof. Fitted [conservatory blinds](/services/conservatory-blinds/) with a reflective backing cut solar heat in summer, and roof blinds hold back a little warmth on winter evenings.
```

Why it works: the situation is caused by the glass, not a fault in the house. The product is described by what it does, and the claim stops at "cut" and "hold back", not a promised temperature.

### Block 4 card, desire led (Made to measure curtains): 40 to 60 words

```
cat_block4:
  - card_title: A bedroom that's light by five in summer | card_body: Light that gets in around a blind is usually coming round the edges, not through the fabric. Lined curtains on a pole that reaches well past the window frame, hung close to the ceiling, cover those gaps. A blackout lining behind the main fabric keeps the look of the curtain while blocking most of the light.
```

Why it works: there is no fault, so the "sign" is a situation. It explains why the light gets in, then the practical fix, and says "most of the light" rather than promising total darkness.

### Block 4 card, situation led by the window (Bay window curtains and blinds): 40 to 60 words

```
cat_block4:
  - card_title: A bay that standard rails won't follow | card_body: Bay windows have angles a straight pole can't follow, so curtains end up on separate poles with gaps at the corners. A track bent on site to the exact shape of the bay lets one pair of curtains run all the way round. Separate blinds for each pane are the other option.
```

Why it works: names the window and the reason ready made options fail, then gives two honest options rather than one sale.

### Block 4 card, problem led (Child safe blinds): 40 to 60 words

```
cat_block4:
  - card_title: Corded blinds in a child's room | card_body: Older blinds with long looped cords or chains are the kind most worth changing in a home with young children. New blinds with cords are usually fitted with a safety device, such as a chain fixed taut to the wall. Cordless or motorised blinds remove the cord altogether, and existing blinds can usually be made safer.
```

Why it works: accurate, calm and stated as what usually applies, with no standard named and no scare. It gives three honest routes, including making existing blinds safer, rather than pushing replacement.

### Service page content block, paragraph 3 (Blind repairs)

```
A roller blind that won't stay up, or drops a few centimetres after every pull, usually has a worn spring or a clutch that has stopped gripping. Left alone, the fabric starts winding unevenly, catches on one side and frays at the edge, and a part that was worth replacing becomes a whole blind. A fabric that still looks good is often worth keeping.
```

Why it works: one concrete consequence the customer would see, with its mechanical cause, written as a reason rather than a scare. It is honest that repair can be the better answer.

### FAQ answer, rules question (Child safe blinds): 50 to 70 words

```
cat_faq_items:
  - faq_question: Do new blinds have to be child safe? | faq_answer: In most cases, yes, for blinds with cords or chains. New blinds sold in the UK are usually designed so cords can't form a dangerous loop, or come with a safety device the fitter fixes in place, such as a chain tensioner. Cordless and motorised blinds avoid cords altogether. Blinds already in a home aren't covered, but can usually have devices added.
```

Why it works: the answer comes first in under 15 words, the rule is written as what usually applies with no standard named, and it separates new blinds from existing ones honestly. (Any detail comes from the trade facts pack on the day; never from this example.)

### Location page content block, paragraph 1 (town)

```
Fitting blinds and curtains in [town] depends on which part of town the house is in. The Edwardian and 1930s streets were built with bay windows, which need curtain tracks bent to shape or a blind for every pane. On the newer estates, window reveals are often plasterboard over steel lintels, so brackets need fixings chosen for what's actually behind the plaster.
```

Why it works: two town facts, each tied to what it means for the fitting. It describes how the houses were built, not the state of anyone's windows, and explains no single service.

## 6. Blog posts and cost guides

Blog posts are the longest prose on the site and the place this trade's knowledge shows most. The voice and the local detail rules above still apply, with one important difference.

**No situations on the blog.** On the website pages, desire led services are written as the customer's situation. The blog prompt bans scenarios and imagined customers, so on the blog a desire led topic is written as a *decision* instead: what each option suits, and what decides between them. "Roman blinds or curtains for a bedroom?" not "Imagine waking up in a perfectly dark room".

**What each post type is for in this trade:**

- **Question posts**: light round a blackout blind, inside or outside the recess, keeping a conservatory cool, blinds for tilt and turn windows, making corded blinds child safe. Answer with the reason in the window or the fitting, then the fix.
- **Decision posts**: blinds or curtains, Roman or roller, wooden Venetian or faux wood in a bathroom, shutters or blinds for a street facing room, motorised or manual, made to measure or ready made. Say which option suits which window and room, never which is the right one for everyone.
- **Cost guides**: what moves the price in this trade. Window size and number, the shape (bays, roof windows and conservatory roofs take longer), fabric and lining, the mechanism, shutter material, and fitting height. Explain why each one moves the price. Figures only from the PRICE rows.
- **Local and seasonal posts**: one housing or light condition in one named place, and what it means for the work: bays on interwar streets, roof windows where lofts are converted, low winter sun.

### Blog answer (question post): 40 to 60 words

```
blog_answer: Blackout blinds still let light in because it comes round the edges, not through the fabric. A roller blind hung inside the recess always has a small gap at each side for the mechanism. Fitting it outside the recess with a wide overlap, or choosing a blind that runs in side channels, closes most of that gap.
```

Why it works: the first sentence answers on its own and makes a clean meta description. The cause is physical and the fixes are practical options, not a sale.

### Blog section (question post): 140 to 200 words

```
blog_sections:
- h2: Should blinds go inside or outside the window recess? | body: Blinds go inside the recess when it is deep enough and outside when it isn't. Inside the recess looks neater and leaves the sill clear, but the blind needs enough depth to sit clear of the window handles, and that depth varies a lot between houses.\n\nOutside the recess suits shallow reveals, windows with handles that stick out, and rooms where light control matters most. The blind sits on the wall above the window and overlaps the opening on each side, which blocks far more light at the edges. It also makes a small window look larger when it is raised above the frame.\n\nThe wall decides as much as the look. Brackets above a window go into the lintel or the plaster over it, and steel lintels, concrete lintels and plasterboard each need different fixings. Blinds that sit inside a uPVC window frame, with no drilling into the wall, are another option on modern windows. Our [made to measure blinds](/services/made-to-measure-blinds/) page explains how each kind is measured.
```

Why it works: the section opens by answering its H2, each paragraph carries one fact that would come from the trade facts rows (recess depth against handles, overlap and light, fixing into lintels), and it treats the choice as practical rather than taste. The link sits in the closing paragraph as natural text.

**What fails on the blog in this trade:** interior trend posts any national site could write; "it depends" without naming the window, recess or room; DIY instructions for working at height or wiring motorised blinds into the mains; child safety posts that frighten rather than inform.

GBP descriptions, services and products don't load this pack: their prompts are built from a facts block in code.

## 7. What makes copy fail in this trade

- A sentence that could sit on any blinds company's site in the country, with no window, recess, light or fixing detail anywhere in the paragraph.
- Problems invented for desire led services ("signs your home needs new blinds").
- Lifestyle and showroom language ("transform your space", "luxurious elegance") instead of what the blind or curtain does in that window.
- Promising total blackout, a set temperature drop or a cut in heating bills.
- Child safety written as fear, as blame, with a standard number, or as a promise that a product is "completely safe".
- Local detail used as scenery: an era named with nothing about the windows it was built with or how they are fitted.
- Claims about what is in people's windows today, or that an area's homes are draughty or dated.
- Free measuring visits, home showrooms, fitting guarantees or lead times stated as this client's practice when not confirmed.
- Brand names for fitted frame or roof window systems instead of the plain description.
