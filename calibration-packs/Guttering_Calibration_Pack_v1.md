## 1. The trade and its reader

Two different customers read a guttering site, and the copy has to know which one it is talking to.

- **The clearing customer** has noticed water pouring over the gutter in rain, a plant growing out of it, a damp patch on the wall below, or a downpipe that gurgles and backs up. They are buying a visit that is quick, tidy and safe, at a height they can't reach themselves. Their fear is a cowboy who clears the front, ignores the back, or leaves the mess on the drive. Copy for them is short and practical: what blocks gutters, what it leads to, what a proper clear covers.
- **The replacement customer** has gutters that leak at every joint, sag between brackets, or roofline boards that are rotten, flaking or stained. They are spending real money once and their fear is new plastic fitted over old rot, or a run that drips at the joints within two winters. Copy for them talks about what is behind and under the gutter (the board, the brackets, the fall), because that is where cheap work cuts corners.

The voice is a roofline fitter who works at height every day, explaining plainly where the water goes and why it matters to the wall below. Never a salesperson pitching "a fresh new look".

## 2. How this trade's services sort into the template

Code chooses the business type and the block briefs. This section tells you what each block should be *about* for each part of this trade, so the copy fits the brief.

| Category (GBP) | Type | Block 4 is about |
|---|---|---|
| Gutter Cleaning Service (Gutter cleaning, Downpipes, Gutter guards and leaf protection) | A project, schedule led | What fills a gutter and when, and what slips when it isn't cleared |
| Roofer, shared pages (Gutter repairs, Guttering installation, Aluminium guttering, Cast iron guttering, Commercial guttering) | A project | Symptom, cause, consequence: dripping joints, sagging runs, water over the front edge |
| Roofer, shared pages (Fascias and soffits, Bargeboards, Cladding, Dry ridge and dry verge) | A project | Symptoms in the boards and verges, and what they lead to behind the gutter |

**Schedule led, inside type A.** This trade's lead service is a clear, and clearing is a schedule: gutters fill steadily from leaves, moss and silt, and the job is to empty them before they overflow. So the gutter cleaning cards are written as what slips and when, not as single faults. It stays type A rather than B because most customers book a single visit when they notice a problem, and few run a round. The schedule led framing gives the card its timing without promising this client a visiting rhythm.

**The split inside type A.**

- **Schedule led** (write what slips and when): Gutter cleaning, Downpipes, Gutter guards and leaf protection, and commercial gutter clearing where confirmed.
- **Problem led** (write symptom, cause, consequence): Gutter repairs, Fascias and soffits, Bargeboards, Dry ridge and dry verge, Cast iron guttering repairs, Cladding where it has failed.
- **Desire led** (write the situation): Guttering installation as an upgrade, Aluminium guttering, and replacing a whole roofline when the customer wants to stop repainting it. Nobody has symptoms of wanting aluminium; they want a run that lasts and doesn't need painting.

**Seasonal niche.** Gutter clearing is seasonal. Where code swaps a block 4 card for the time of year, write the reason in the trees and the weather: gutters usually want clearing after the leaves are down in late autumn and before the heavy winter rain, a second clear can make sense in spring under trees that drop seeds and blossom, and moss washed off a roof builds up through the wet months. Never "autumn is a busy time".

## 3. Local detail that works in this trade

Gutters take whatever falls on the roof, so tree cover and housing era are the strongest local detail.

**Strong anchors** (area wide and checkable): tree cover and mature trees close to houses; shaded roofs where moss builds up; exposure to wind and driven rain; housing era and what it was usually built with (cast iron gutters and timber fascias on Victorian and Edwardian terraces; uPVC rooflines first fitted widely in the 1990s, now at an age when brackets give and boards discolour; flats and bungalows with long, low runs); conservation areas where the research confirms cast iron must be replaced like for like; access (three storey townhouses, conservatories under the gutter, narrow side passages).

**Every anchor must change the work.** Tree cover is only worth mentioning if it then explains how often a gutter fills. A Victorian terrace is only worth mentioning if it then explains cast iron and the care its joints need. A 1990s estate is only worth mentioning if it then explains why replacement means stripping the old boards, not capping over them.

**Do not:** claim gutters in an area are blocked, leaking or failing; turn an era into a claim about current state ("the original fascias still fitted", "rooflines here are rotting"); write "built with" as if it were "still has"; invent storm or flood claims the research doesn't support.

## 4. Craft rules for this trade

1. **Follow the water.** Every fault in this trade is water going somewhere it shouldn't: over the front edge onto the wall, behind the gutter into the fascia, down a cracked downpipe into the brickwork, into the ground at the footings. Say where it goes and what it does there, and the reader sees why a gutter matters.
2. **Timing is the clear.** When gutters fill, and when they need emptying, is the trade's main idea. Most good clearing cards and FAQs have a time of year and a reason in the trees or the weather.
3. **A proper clear includes the downpipes.** Emptying the gutter while the outlet or downpipe stays blocked moves the problem a few feet. Good copy says the whole run is cleared and the water is checked flowing, written as the principle of a good job, not this business's process.
4. **Strip, don't cap.** Capping fits new uPVC over old timber boards and hides whatever is rotting behind. Stripping the old boards out lets the rafter ends be checked and the new boards fixed to sound timber. This is the roofline buyer's fear and the copy should meet it head on, consistent with the Roofer pack.
5. **Brackets and falls, not just the gutter.** A run that sags or drips at the joints has usually lost a bracket or its fall, so water sits and works the seals. Name the bracket and the fall, and the copy reads as a fitter.
6. **Height is never DIY.** Copy never tells the reader to clear gutters from a ladder, lean on a gutter, or step onto a conservatory roof. It says the work is at height and is done with the right access.

## 5. What good looks like: one example per field type

### Homepage service card (type A schedule led, Gutter cleaning): 40 to 55 words

```
home_services_cards:
  - card_name: Gutter cleaning | card_text: Water spilling over the gutter in heavy rain usually means it filled with leaves and moss months earlier. Our [gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning/) clears the gutters, outlets and downpipes along the whole run, and a good clear is timed after the leaves are down, so the gutters are empty for the winter rain.
```

Why it works: opens on what the customer sees, puts the cause months earlier, names what a full clear covers, and carries the timing that makes this schedule led.

### Category page service card (type A, Fascias and soffits): 40 to 55 words

```
cat_services_cards:
  - card_name: Fascias and soffits | card_text: New uPVC fitted over old timber boards looks finished on the day and hides whatever is rotting behind it. Our [fascias and soffits](/services/fascias-and-soffits/) work strips the old boards out first, so the rafter ends can be checked and the new boards fixed to sound timber.
```

Why it works: names the cheap shortcut the customer fears and the hidden layer behind it, describing the principle of good work rather than a claimed process.

### Block 4 card, schedule led (Gutter Cleaning Service): 40 to 60 words

```
cat_block4:
  - card_title: Gutters under trees | card_body: Gutters near trees fill fastest in autumn, and a wet layer of leaves left over winter turns to silt that blocks the outlet. Once the outlet goes, water spills over the front edge and down the wall. A clear once the leaves are down usually keeps the gutters running through the heavy rain.
```

Why it works: a time of year, a reason in the trees, and the consequence of missing it, with no visit frequency claimed.

### Block 4 card, problem led (Gutter repairs): 40 to 60 words

```
cat_block4:
  - card_title: Drips at the joints | card_body: A gutter that drips at the joints but isn't blocked has usually lost its fall, often because a bracket has given. Water then sits in the run and works the rubber seals loose. Left alone, it soaks the wall below and the fascia behind, so a new bracket and reset fall usually fixes it.
```

Why it works: symptom, cause and consequence in the order the customer meets them, and the fix is a repair, not a replacement pitch.

### Block 4 card, desire led (Guttering installation): 40 to 60 words

```
cat_block4:
  - card_title: Tired of repainting the roofline | card_body: If the timber fascias and gutters need painting every few years, replacing the whole roofline in one go usually ends that cycle. Stripping the old boards, fitting new fascias and soffits, then a new gutter run on fresh brackets means every part is fixed to sound timber at the same time.
```

Why it works: there is no fault, so the "sign" is a situation the reader recognises, and the answer is practical, consistent with strip rather than cap.

### Service page content block, paragraph 3 (Gutter cleaning)

```
A gutter that overflows sends rainwater down the face of the wall in the same place every storm. Over a few winters that soaks the brickwork, shows as damp or mould on the inside wall, and can wash out mortar or stain render. At ground level the water pools against the footings instead of reaching the drain.
```

Why it works: follows the water from the gutter to the wall to the ground, one consequence with its cause, written as a reason rather than a scare.

### FAQ answer (Gutter cleaning): 50 to 70 words

```
cat_faq_items:
  - faq_question: How often should gutters be cleaned? | faq_answer: Usually once a year, in late autumn after the leaves have fallen. Houses under or near trees often need a second clear in spring, when seeds and blossom fill the gutters again. Roofs with heavy moss shed it into the gutters steadily, so they tend to fill faster too. Water spilling over the front edge in rain is the sign a gutter is already full.
```

Why it works: the answer comes first in under 15 words, written as what usually applies, then names the conditions that change it and the sign to watch for.

### Location page content block, paragraph 1 (town)

```
Guttering in [town] follows the trees and the age of the housing. The roads around the park are lined with mature trees, so gutters there fill each autumn and need clearing before winter. The Victorian terraces near the centre were built with cast iron gutters and timber fascias, while the 1990s estates to the east were among the first to have uPVC rooflines fitted, now at an age when brackets give.
```

Why it works: two town facts, each tied to what it means for the work. The era claims say what the houses were built with, not what they have now.

## 6. Blog posts and cost guides

Blog posts are the longest prose on the site and the place this trade's knowledge shows most. The voice and the local detail rules above still apply, with one important difference.

**No situations on the blog.** On the website pages, desire led services are written as the customer's situation. The blog prompt bans scenarios and imagined customers, so on the blog a desire led topic is written as a *decision* instead: what each option suits, and what decides between them. "Aluminium or uPVC guttering?" not "Imagine never painting again".

**What each post type is for in this trade:**

- **Question posts**: why gutters overflow in heavy rain, why a gutter drips at the joints, why there's a damp patch under the gutter, whether gutter guards work, what the black streaks on uPVC fascias are. Answer with where the water goes and why, then what fixes it.
- **Decision posts**: repair or replace a gutter run; uPVC, aluminium or cast iron; gutter guards or regular clearing; strip or cap over old fascias; repair or replace cast iron on a period house. Say which suits which house, never which wins.
- **Cost guides**: what moves the price. Height and access (storeys, conservatories, side passages), the length of the run, how many downpipes, whether boards need stripping, material, and taking the old roofline away. For clearing: height, how full the gutters are, and whether scaffold or a tower is needed. Explain why each moves the price. Figures only from the PRICE rows.
- **Local and seasonal posts**: one tree, weather or housing condition in one named place and what it means for gutters at that time of year. Tree lined streets and autumn clearing, cast iron on period streets, exposure and driven rain.

### Blog answer (question post): 40 to 60 words

```
blog_answer: Gutters overflow in heavy rain usually because the outlet or downpipe is blocked, not because the gutter is too small. Leaves and moss wash to the outlet and settle there as silt, so water backs up along the run and spills over the front edge. Clearing the outlet and downpipe as well as the gutter fixes most cases.
```

Why it works: the first sentence answers on its own and makes a clean meta description. The cause is physical, and the answer corrects the common belief.

### Blog section (decision post): 140 to 200 words

```
blog_sections:
- h2: Should old fascias be stripped or capped? | body: Stripping old fascias is usually the better choice, because capping hides whatever is wrong behind them. Capping fits a thin uPVC board over the existing timber, which is quicker and avoids disturbing the roof edge, but any rot in the old board carries on out of sight.\n\nStripping takes the old boards off completely. That exposes the rafter ends, so any that have softened can be repaired before the new fascias are fixed. It also lets the bottom row of tiles and the felt at the eaves be checked, and soffit ventilation added where the roof space needs it.\n\nCapping can make sense when the timber underneath is sound, dry and firmly fixed, and only a survey of the boards can confirm that. Where the old boards are already soft or flaking, capping simply covers a problem that will spread. Our [fascias and soffits](/services/fascias-and-soffits/) page explains what replacement involves.
```

Why it works: the section opens by answering its H2, each paragraph carries one fact the trade facts rows would supply (what capping does, what stripping exposes, when capping can work), and it is honest about the case for the cheaper option. The link sits in the closing paragraph.

**What fails on the blog in this trade:** DIY gutter clearing guides involving ladders or roofs; posts that treat gutter guards as a permanent fix with no mention of clearing them; "it depends" without naming the trees, the height or the material; seasonal posts that name a month with no reason in the trees or the weather.

GBP descriptions, services and products don't load this pack: their prompts are built from a facts block in code.

## 7. What makes copy fail in this trade

- A sentence that could sit on any guttering site in the country, with no water path, bracket, board or timing anywhere in the paragraph.
- Gutter cleaning copy that ignores outlets and downpipes.
- Capping over old boards presented as equal to stripping them.
- Era claims written as current state ("rooflines here are rotting", "still fitted with the original gutters").
- Clearing frequencies, before and after photos or waste removal stated as this client's practice when not confirmed.
- Any suggestion the reader should clear gutters from a ladder or stand on a conservatory roof.
- Gutter guards sold as a permanent end to clearing.
- Water damage overstated into a scare (subsidence, collapse) instead of what it actually does to walls and boards.
- Lifestyle language ("a fresh new look") on roofline replacement instead of what is fixed to what.
- Symptoms invented for desire led upgrades ("signs you need aluminium gutters").
