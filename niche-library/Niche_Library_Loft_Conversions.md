Format: Homepage Structure, Layer 2, section 3 (Niche slot entry).

## Steps (type A)

Each step has a fixed title and a one line brief the model writes to.

1. **Tell Us About Your House and the Room You Need**
   Brief: How to get in touch and what is useful to send first: the kind of house (a terrace, a semi, a bungalow, a flat), what the new room is for, photos of the loft from the hatch or of the back of the house, drawings if they already have them. No promise of a reply time unless [CV: response_time] is set.
2. **We Visit, Measure and Price the Job** (only when the `site_visit_before_quote` practice is confirmed)
   Brief: The visit and what gets looked at on site: the height under the ridge and how the roof is built (a cut roof with purlins, or trusses crossing the loft), where a stair could land and what it takes from the floor below, the side return or the garden and where the drains run, how a scaffold and a skip get to the house, whether the job touches a wall the neighbour shares, and what the planning position looks like for that house (looked at on the day, never settled on the day). Follows [CV: quote_method]; only say the quote is written if quote_method says so. Never restates 'free' (FAQ 5 owns it). Never a free survey, a fixed price, a start date or a verdict that the loft will convert.
   Without the practice (`step_2_no_visit`): **We Price Your Job**
   Brief: Say a price is agreed before any work starts. Describe how the price is reached only from [CV: quote_method]; when quote_method is blank, name no method at all (no photos, call, video or visit). Only say the quote is written if quote_method says so. Never restates 'free' (FAQ 5 owns it). Never a verdict that a loft will convert before the house has been seen.
3. **We Start on a Date That Suits You**
   Brief: The start date is agreed in advance, to suit the customer. Never mention the price, the quote or changes to the plan (step 2 and Why choose us own them). Never restates the start time (FAQ 4 owns it). No finish date, duration or week by week plan: the trade facts give none. One line on the order of work only as a principle (a loft is built from the scaffold outside and the ceiling is opened for the stair late; an extension is made weathertight before the wall into the house comes down), never as this business's timetable.

## Planning and extensions: the positions this entry takes

The pack's section 2 says its extensions, planning and building regulations block is identical in the Builder and Loft Conversions packs. These are the Builder entry's twelve positions, one line each, as this entry takes them, followed by every point where the loft conversions facts pack made this entry say more or say it differently.

1. Two approvals, never blurred: planning decides whether a thing may be built (how big, how high, how it looks, the neighbours); building regulations decide whether it is built safely (the structure and the new floor, foundations, the way down in a fire, insulation, drains, ventilation); every brief that mentions one says which one it means.
2. Permitted development is written as what usually applies, never as a promise: a single floor added behind the house, a loft with a dormer at the back or roof lights that keeps the roof as it is, and a garage made into a room inside its own walls usually need no planning application.
3. What more often or more likely needs an application: a second floor added, building out beside the house or round its corner, a dormer facing the road, any flat or maisonette, a listed home, a home in a conservation area, and an estate where the council has withdrawn the usual allowance.
4. The certificate: a lawful development certificate is written as "a certificate from the council is the usual way to confirm that a job needs none", never by its name, never as compulsory.
5. Building control: every loft conversion and nearly every extension and garage conversion needs building regulations approval whether or not planning was needed; building control looks at the work as it goes (the bottom of the trench before concrete is the one inspection named for an extension) and issues a completion certificate at the end, which is a record of the sign off and never a planning permission, a warranty or a guarantee.
6. The exemptions: a conservatory on the ground floor kept separate from the house by outside quality doors and a small detached garden building nobody sleeps in are often outside building control; open the conservatory into the house and it is treated as an extension; said as "often", with no area, height or distance.
7. Party walls: work on a shared wall, or footings dug close to a neighbour's, usually means the neighbour is told in writing before work starts and can agree or have a surveyor act for them; it is separate from both approvals; on a terrace or a semi a loft's new floor and its beams usually sit on that shared wall; never the Act's name, never a notice period, a distance or a depth.
8. Lofts: a rear dormer or a roof light conversion that keeps the roof as it is usually needs no application; a dormer facing the road, a mansard, and most loft work in a conservation area usually do; every loft needs building regulations approval, including a protected way down from the new floor; no volume or projection figure.
9. Annexes and basements: planning usually draws a line between an annex the household itself uses and one someone lives in or lets on their own, which usually needs permission; converting an existing cellar usually needs none, digging a new basement usually does; no figure.
10. Never a figure, an area, a depth, a height, a volume, a distance or a notice period; never an Act's, an order's, a class's or an approved document's name; never "no permission needed" stated before the house has been seen; the planning position is looked at on the site visit and never settled there.
11. Who does it: the application, the notice and the engineer are arranged by this business only from [CV: handles_planning] and the confirmed practices; otherwise the copy says they are a stage of the job agreed with the customer, and never implies design, engineering or drawing capability.
12. Local claims: a conservation area, a removed allowance or green belt is named only where the town research confirms it, and only when it changes which approval the job needs; otherwise the general position and the site visit.

Where the loft conversions facts pack made this entry differ from, or add to, the builder's:

- **Position 3 and 8, the mansard.** The builder says a mansard usually needs an application. Table C says a mansard has no automatic exemption of its own: its design is checked against the same allowance as any other roof enlargement, and gets permission where it does not fit. The briefs here keep "usually needs one" and add "checked against the same allowance rather than assumed to fit".
- **Position 3 and 8, a raised roof and a flat.** Table E adds that a roof raised above its existing ridge cannot rely on the usual loft allowance, and that the loft allowance belongs to houses and not to flats or maisonettes. Both are in the planning briefs as "more likely to need one"; the builder's list had the flat for extensions only.
- **Position 2, the deeper rear extension.** Table C and E give a lighter route for a single storey rear extension deeper than the usual allowance, where the council is told and asks the neighbours before work can start. The builder's positions do not mention it. The service rules FAQ says "a deeper one sometimes goes through a lighter route where the council asks the neighbours first", with no depth and no period.
- **Position 6, the conservatory's heating.** Table C and E make the conservatory's exemption depend on its own heating as well as the outside doors: extend the house heating into it and the exemption goes. The briefs say "kept separate from the house by outside doors, with its own heating".
- **Position 7, the award is not automatic.** Table E says a party wall award is not automatic where the neighbour agrees, and that not every semi detached loft needs one. The location card says "never that every loft needs a surveyor". Table C also lets building control ask for the wall between two lofts to be built up for sound; the location card says "sometimes".
- **Position 8, the fire route.** Table C, E and G add what the builder's pack did not spell out: for a loft that makes a third floor the whole route down is protected, with fire doors on the rooms off the stair upgraded only where they need to be (not every door, not a blanket rating), linked alarms, and an escape window alone does not replace that route for that arrangement; an open plan ground floor needs a specific alternative design rather than being a bar. The briefs say it in plain words with no rating, grade, height or pitch.
- **Position 9, garden rooms.** Table C and E add that a garden building nobody sleeps in usually needs no application and that using it as a bedroom or a self contained home changes the planning position. The annex and garden room briefs carry it.
- **Position 5, the public sewer.** Table C and E add the water company's say where an extension stands over or close to a public sewer, as something checked rather than an automatic agreement. The cost and What's Included briefs say "the water company usually has to agree", with no distance.
- **Position 10, sign off after the event.** Table C adds that the council can look at earlier unapproved work and certify it, sometimes after alterations, and that an indemnity policy is not a sign off. The fallback problem led card says so without the procedure's name or its date.
- **Trusses, the hip and the stair.** Not planning, but the pack's craft rules 1 and 2 and Table A, B and E give this entry two constraint cards the builder does not have (what the roof allows; where the stair lands and what it takes from the floor below) and the line that existing ceiling joists are not a floor. The builder's "a hipped roof points to one kind of loft conversion and a trussed roof to a different, bigger one" is said here in full.
