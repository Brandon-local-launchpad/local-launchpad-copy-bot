## Steps (type A)

Each step has a fixed title and a one line brief the model writes to.

1. **Tell Us What You've Seen or Heard**
   Brief: How to get in touch and what is useful to say first: what they have seen or heard (droppings, scratching, insects going in and out of one spot, bites, holes in timber or in clothes) and where and when (at night, in the daytime, in the loft, behind the cooker, under the floor); whether anyone in the house reacts badly to stings; whether there are pets or small children, because that shapes what is used and where (the trade facts: bait kept from children and animals that are not the target, hosts treated separately for fleas); a photo taken from a distance if it is safe, since the insect or animal is identified before anything is treated (Table E: a cluster of honey bees can be a swarm, not a wasp nest). Nothing from the shop meanwhile, with the reason from Table B only: a nest disturbed without proper control means more stings, trails treated without the colony leave the source, bait left in the open puts pets and other animals at risk. Never how to use any product. No promise of a reply time unless [CV: response_time] is set.
2. **We Identify the Pest and Price the Job** (only when the `site_visit_before_quote` practice is confirmed)
   Brief: The visit and what gets looked at: which insect or animal it is (every Table A row starts there: wasp against bee, rat against mouse, active against historic woodworm), where it lives and how it gets in (harbourage, food, water and entry points; the drain where rat entry is suspected; the roof line and the vents for squirrels and birds), and what has to be weighed before anything is used (children, pets, other animals, a protected animal or a nest in use, access and height). Then the price. On a survey page the survey is the job that is priced, and any treatment is priced off what it finds. Follows [CV: quote_method]; only say the quote is written if quote_method says so. Never restates 'free' (FAQ 5 owns it).
   Without the practice (`step_2_no_visit`): **We Price Your Job**
   Brief: Say a price is agreed before any work starts. Describe how the price is reached only from [CV: quote_method]; when quote_method is blank, name no method at all (no photos, call, video or visit). The insect or animal is still identified on the first visit before anything is used (Table A). Only say the quote is written if quote_method says so. Never restates 'free' (FAQ 5 owns it).
3. **We Treat It and Deal With the Way In**
   Brief: The date is agreed in advance, to suit the customer. What the visit does only from the Table A stages for that pest, in plain words: traps or protected bait points for rodents, the nest treated at its entrance, a treatment or heat for bed bugs, bait the ants carry back to the colony; a follow up visit written as part of the method with its reason in the animal (eggs, pupae or egg cases that a treatment does not reach; traps and bait points that are checked and cleared), never as a fixed number of visits (Table G: none exists); proofing as the last stage where the services list has it, otherwise recommended. Never mention the price, the quote or changes to the plan (step 2 and Why choose us own them). Never restates the start time (FAQ 4 owns it). Never a promise the pest will not return.

### Emergency steps (steps_emergency)

Used on the homepage, the category page and the two emergency service pages when the client confirms call outs and the page is flagged emergency in the library. What the customer does meanwhile comes from the trade facts rows only (Table B: a nest disturbed, exposed bait, accessible food); the pack's "keeping people away from the entrance" holding step is the same thing in other words.

1. **Call Us.** Brief: What to say: what they have seen (a stream of wasps at one spot by a door, a window or where children play; a rat or a mouse seen indoors; droppings in the kitchen), where it is, whether anyone in the house reacts badly to stings, whether there are pets or small children. What to do meanwhile, from the trade facts rows only: keep people and pets away from a nest entrance and leave it alone (Table B: an occupied nest disturbed without proper control means more stings); put food away in sealed containers and clear what has been gnawed (Table B: accessible food keeps rodents active); nothing laid or sprayed from the shop (Table B: exposed bait puts pets and other animals at risk; a body left behind a wall is the smell they fear). Never an instruction to go into the loft or up a ladder. The number from [CV: phone]. No promise of a reply or an arrival time unless [CV: response_time] is set.
2. **We Come Out, Identify It and Agree the Price.** Brief: What usually happens first on the visit, from Table A: the insect or animal confirmed before anything is used (Table E: a cluster of honey bees can be a swarm, and treating the wrong insect harms bees), the nest or the activity located and its exposure assessed, who is at risk and how access is made safe. The customer told what was found. A call out charge only from [CV: quote_call_out_fee]; the price agreed before any treatment only from the price_agreed_before_work practice and [CV: quote_method]. An arrival time only as the business gives it on the call: no window written here unless [CV: response_time] is set; 'same day' only when [CV: same_day_available] is yes. Never restates 'free'.
3. **We Treat It, Then Close the Way In.** Brief: What the visit does, from Table A: a wasp nest treated at its entrance with access made safe and people kept clear, the activity checked afterwards and the empty nest removed only if needed; for rats and mice, traps or protected bait points where justified, follow up visits to check them and to remove spent bait and bodies, then the entry points proofed (Table A stages; Table C: bait points protected, recorded and cleared at the end, as what the professional usually does, never the code's name). The follow up is part of the method with its reason in the animal, never a fixed number of visits. Never promise a one visit fix; never mention the price; never a promise the pest will not return.
